[SECTION 4: 3 MISTAKES TO AVOID - 12:30]

[SCREEN SHOW: "Mistake #1" text on screen]

**Mistake number one: Memorizing facts instead of understanding concepts.**

The AI Associate exam is NOT a memorization test. It's a COMPREHENSION test. The questions are scenario-based. You'll read a paragraph about a fictional company facing a problem, and you have to identify the right solution or explain the right concept.

People who just flashcard-memorize definitions bomb this exam. People who actually understand HOW things work and WHY they work pass it.

So when you study the Trust Layer, don't just memorize "Zero Data Retention = data is not stored." Understand WHY it matters for an enterprise. Understand the business problem it solves. Then you can answer any question about it, even if it's phrased in a way you've never seen before.

[SCREEN SHOW: "Mistake #2" text on screen]

**Mistake number two: Skipping the ethics section because it "seems soft."**

I've heard so many people say, "Ethics? That's easy, it's just common sense." And then they get three ethics questions wrong and miss a passing score by 2 points.

The ethics section has a very specific vocabulary: bias, fairness, transparency, explainability, accountability, human oversight. Salesforce tests SPECIFIC principles and specific scenarios. "A model produces biased outputs" — what's the root cause? Wrong answer: The model is broken. Right answer: Biased training data.

Study the ethics section with the same rigor as the technical sections.

[SCREEN SHOW: "Mistake #3" text on screen]

**Mistake number three: Not doing the hands-on labs.**

I know you can pass the AI Associate without ever logging into a Salesforce org. The exam is mostly conceptual. But here's the thing — when you actually USE Prompt Builder and see the "grounding" concept in action, it becomes real. When you see the Einstein Trust Layer settings in Setup, the concepts stick.

The 30 minutes you spend in a Developer Edition org playing with Prompt Builder will be worth more than 2 hours of reading. Build the case summary template. Try it. See the AI generate output. That experience becomes an anchor for everything you've learned.
